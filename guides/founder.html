Founder onboarding guide

The operating guide for the academy's highest-privilege dashboard.

1. Founder OS

The Founder Command Center is the strategic control surface for academy operations. Use it for decisions, oversight, reports, revenue, people, courses, announcements, security and system intelligence.

2. High-privilege access

Founder access can affect many users and systems. Protect the account like production infrastructure. Never share credentials or leave an authenticated founder session on an unmanaged device.

3. People and roles

Review students, instructors and administrators using role-appropriate tools. Founder and administrator badges identify authority; they are not substitutes for actual permission checks.

4. Revenue and payments

Monitor Supabase payment records, Pesapal references, successful revenue and transaction anomalies. Financial decisions should be supported by verified records rather than UI counters alone.

5. Messaging

The founder can communicate across permitted roles. Use messaging for leadership and operational communication, avoid unnecessary personal data, and remember that messages may be retained for service, security and dispute purposes.

6. Reports and incidents

Review serious reports, security concerns, payment disputes and operational failures. Preserve evidence and avoid destructive edits while an investigation is active.

7. System health

Watch authentication failures, realtime issues, payment webhook health, database errors, unusual traffic and role-security events. Prefer measurable evidence and targeted fixes over broad destructive changes.

8. Governance

Major policy, financial, academic and privacy changes should be documented. Keep terms, privacy policies, support procedures and operational rules aligned with the actual platform.

9. Data minimization

Do not collect information simply because the database can store it. Keep email and internal identifiers private when usernames or role labels are sufficient. Review old data flows and remove unnecessary reads and writes.

10. Safe operations

Use staged changes, migrations, backups and verification. Test payment and role changes with controlled accounts before production rollout. Never bypass authentication or row-level security simply to make a UI work.
